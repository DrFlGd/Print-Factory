# Laser Box

Finger-jointed boxes cut from sheet on a laser: pick a style, set its size and your material,
add a pattern, and download a cut file that fits together. The 3D view shows the box assembled
(and opened, with **3D view: Open**); the Drawing view shows the pieces laid out on your sheets.

## Styles

| Style | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| Open | Four walls and a floor: a tray, a planter sleeve, a lantern. |
| Lift-off lid | The lid sits on the walls; a lip glued under it drops inside them so it cannot slide off. A thumb notch in the front wall lets a finger under it. |
| Hinged lid | The lid sits between the side walls, flush with the top. Square pins at its back corners turn in round holes in the side walls, under a round knuckle. A tab on its front edge drops into a recess in the front wall: it holds the lid shut, and its overhang is the pull. |
| Drawers | A case with one to three bays and a drawer in each. Each drawer's front is an overlay plate that covers the case's edges and stops it when pushed home; its sides and floor tab through the plate. |

Give the **outside** size, or the **inside** size (the space in the box; in a chest, in each
drawer).

## Bottom

- **Flush:** the floor is finger-jointed into the walls' bottom edges.
- **Inset:** the floor sits a sheet up, its tabs through slots in the walls.
- **Feet:** the floor sits higher and the walls are cut away between feet at the corners (and
  in the middle of long walls).

**Dividers** stand on the floor in an egg-crate, tabbed through slots in the walls and the
floor, halved into each other where they cross.

## Material and fit

Pick the material, and type the thickness you measured if your sheet is not quite nominal. The
kerf (how wide the laser cuts) follows the material unless you set it: half of it is added round
every cut outline, so the pieces come out the size they were drawn.

**Fit** is how hard joints press together. Cut a single corner first; if it is loose or tight,
move Fit, not the kerf. **Flex tabs** are wider tabs with two slits each wherever a tab goes
into a slot (a raised floor, a shelf, a divider, a drawer front): the slits let them press home
by hand, no glue. Corners are always plain fingers. Acrylic and greyboard take plain fingers
only.

## Patterns

Asanoha, seigaiha, shippo, hexagons, diamonds, stars, dots or waves on the lid, the front, the
walls, or all of them. **Score** draws the lines; **engrave** fills the cells; **cut through**
makes a lattice, keeping at least the **web** of wood between cut-outs. A pattern keeps its
**margin** clear of every joint, hole and hinge. Give it a **shape** (a heart, a circle, a star)
to fill only that, with an optional scored **frame** round it. On a lift-off lid a cut pattern
stays inside the lip's window.

## Files

SVG (millimetres, one group per laser operation: cut red, score blue, engrave black) or DXF,
one file per sheet when the pieces need more than one (a zip). Plywood pieces are all laid the
same way round so the grain matches.
