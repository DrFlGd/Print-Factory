# House Number

A house or office sign: a number, an optional second line, a plate, and hidden mounts.

## Text

- **First line** is usually the number; **Second line** a street, family or room name at
  its own size, above, below, left or right of the first.
- **Stacked** puts one character above the next, for door frames and posts.
- **Overall size** scales the whole face together (margins, corners, gaps) while the
  thickness and screw sizes stay as set, so a template can be made bigger without
  re-tuning it.

## Plate

Rounded, square, pill, plaque (cut corners) or **no plate**, where the characters are the
sign and a bridge through each row holds them together. A fixed width or height makes a
run of signs match; a size too small for the text grows to fit.

Text can be **raised**, **inlaid** (flush, a second filament; print face down for the
smoothest finish) or **engraved**. Plate details add a colour strip along one edge, a
raised panel under the text, a raised frame, and a bevel on the front edge.

## Mounting

- **Keyholes:** slots in the back that hang on two screws, so nothing shows on the front.
  They need a plate about 5.5 mm thick or more.
- **Screw holes:** two or four, countersunk from the front.
- **Tape:** a flat back for outdoor double-sided tape or adhesive.

For outdoor signs print in PETG or ASA; PLA softens in direct sun.
