# Cable Organiser

Cable organisers sized to the cables you have: measure a cable (or pick a typical size from
the hint) and every ring fits it.

## What it makes

- **Cable holder:** a row of snap-in rings on a flat back. Stick it on with double-sided
  foam tape, or choose screws for a countersunk hole at each end (for 4 mm screws).
- **Desk-edge clip:** a C that pushes onto the edge of a desk top, set to its thickness, with
  the rings hanging under the lower jaw. Small lips at the jaw tips hold it on; the jaws
  spring about a millimetre to go on, which PETG takes better than PLA.
- **Cable winder:** a flat card with a waist to wind earbuds or a charger round, and a slit
  at each end that grips the cable's ends.
- **Cable tag:** a small flag with a raised label and a ring that clips onto the cable. The
  text stands 0.6 mm proud, so a filament change at 1.6 mm prints it in a second colour.

## The snap rings

Each ring's bore is 0.3 mm wider than the cable. Its mouth is narrower than the cable (85 %
of it for a light grip, 72 % for a firm one) and opens out at 45° so the cable finds it.
The holder and the clip are drawn as a profile and print lying on it, so the rings flex
along the layers rather than across them and do not snap off.

## Printing

Everything prints without supports. Print the holder and the clip as they are placed, on
their side. PLA works for the holder, the winder and the tag; PETG is better for the desk
clip, which has to spring open.
