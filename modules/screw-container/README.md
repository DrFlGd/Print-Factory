# Screw-Top Container

Containers whose lids screw on, made to measure: kitchen and spice jars, bottles, keyring
capsules, and towers of tiers that screw into each other. Everything prints without
supports.

## Jars

- **Shape:** round, hexagonal, octagonal or square, with the width measured across the
  flats and the corners rounded as you like.
- **Wide mouth:** the lid is as wide as the jar and sits flush on a ledge. On a polygon jar
  the lid has the same shape and lines up with the sides when it is screwed shut.
- **Narrow neck:** the jar rises at 45° to a collar and a smaller neck, like a bottle. The
  lid is round and sits on the collar.
- **Dividers:** two to six walls inside a wide-mouth jar, from the middle out.
- **Lid:** smooth, knurled or ribbed when it is round. Its top can carry an engraved label.
  A taller lid becomes a cup of its own (a capsule's lid). A keyring loop can go on top; that
  lid prints opening down, so keep it small.

## Stacks

Each tier has its male thread on a short hollow spigot underneath and its female thread in
the rim at the top, so tiers screw into each other in any order. The bottom tier has a flat
base, and a lid caps the top. Where two tiers meet, the spigot widens at 45° into a matching
countersink in the rim below, and the tier then widens to its full width, also at 45°. That
keeps the tiers printable without supports. The trade-off is a groove at each joint, which
is deep at the corners of a square.

## The thread

- **Pitch:** fine (2 mm), standard (3 mm) or coarse (4 mm). The flanks are 45°, so the
  thread prints cleanly on the outside of a neck and inside a lid.
- **Starts:** one, two or three threads side by side. Two or three close in fewer turns;
  the summary tells you how many.
- **Thread height:** how far the thread runs up the neck. It is at least three pitches.
- **Fit:** the clearance between the threads. Use tight (0.2 mm) for a well-tuned printer,
  standard (0.3 mm) otherwise, and loose (0.45 mm) if threads bind.

The neck's thread fades in and out at its ends so it starts easily. The lid's groove is the
same thread, widened by the clearance.

## Printing

- Jars and tiers print standing up. Lids print upside down on their tops, so an engraved
  label comes out crisp.
- Inside a jar, the walls narrow to the mouth at 45°. The summary gives the mouth's width
  and how much the jar holds.
- PETG or PLA both work. For food, use a food-safe material and remember that layer lines
  hold dirt.
